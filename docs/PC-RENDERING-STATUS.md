# PC rendering status

This is a work-in-progress record of the high-fidelity PC rendering pass. It is not a release or performance acceptance report.

## Current configuration

- The Standalone default quality level is **PC**, using `Assets/Settings/PC_RPAsset.asset` and the Universal Render Pipeline. Player color space is Linear. The PC pipeline asset enables HDR, depth and opaque textures, a 2048 main-light shadow map, four shadow cascades, soft shadows and the SRP Batcher. See `ProjectSettings/QualitySettings.asset`, `ProjectSettings/ProjectSettings.asset` and `Assets/Settings/PC_RPAsset.asset` under `RailArena`.
- The PC renderer includes an enabled screen-space ambient occlusion feature. The Sky Gardens grading profile contains ACES tonemapping, bloom, color grading and camera-only motion blur. These are configuration facts; they are not a performance or artistic acceptance claim.
- Sky Gardens has baked lighting and light probes in its generated scene. Other maps and dynamic objects need their own validation.
- Under **Settings > Frame Pacing**, use **Graphics** to cycle High, Balanced and Performance. The choice saves immediately; High is the default and retains the PC asset's current settings. Balanced uses 0.9 render scale, 40 m shadows, four cascades, 2048 shadow resolution and 1.5 LOD bias. Performance uses 0.75 render scale, 30 m shadows, two cascades, 1024 shadow resolution and 1.0 LOD bias. These tiers have not yet been benchmarked or visually accepted.
- VFX Graph 17.6.0 is installed to match URP 17.6.0. Package installation alone does not add a GPU effect; the existing traversal presentation still uses its previous implementation.
- Isolated candidates based on Unity's burst template live in `Assets/Validation/TraversalVfxCandidate`. Graphics-enabled, offscreen Play Mode tests observed 128 and 2,048 live GPU particles respectively after an explicit play event; neither graph started before the event. A test-only bridge also responds to the real Sky Gardens portal presentation activation, respects its cooldown and suppresses the burst with reduced flash enabled. The prototype is not attached in a playable scene; production integration, world-physics response, performance and visual review remain open.
- An initial portal capture showed the unmodified high-capacity template as a dense orange obstruction. A smaller, translucent cyan candidate was then captured from the playable side and passed the same activation and reduced-flash test. The capture is a single frame, not a motion or gameplay-readability acceptance.
- Matching offscreen captures of the existing effect and the cyan candidate at approximately 0.15, 0.35 and 0.65 seconds show little clearly distinguishable added particle detail at the tested distance. The prototype should not be integrated on particle count alone; improve the effect's contribution and measure its cost first. The comparison and timing logs are in `Validation/SkyGardens-Playable-2026-10-29/TraversalVfxCaptures` and `traversal-vfx-timeline-compare.log`.
- A brighter, larger particle variant is more visible at five metres, but centered emission obscures much of the portal at 2.5 metres. Raising the test emitter above the frame keeps the center clearer in sampled frames, though particles still cross the upper view and require motion, readability and cost review. It remains a test-only candidate.
- A hidden Editor timing probe alternated the existing portal and the prototype twice. Repeated prototype activations reached the graph's 4,096-particle capacity, but all four runs returned zero valid GPU timing samples. In the latest report, a median of `-1` means unavailable, not a measurement of GPU cost; see `traversal-vfx-timing-20261030-031018-810.json`.
- A separate VFX graph with a fixed test plane passed a controlled GPU collision comparison: visible pixels below the plane fell from 2,689 without collision to zero with collision at the sampled time. This demonstrates the prototype plane block, not collision against actual arena geometry.
- A further test-only graph exposes its plane position and normal. Controlled GPU tests confirmed the collision boundary moves with translation and tilts with a sloped normal; a Sky Gardens test bound position and normal to a surface found by raycast near a portal. This is a single-plane approximation, not collision against all arena geometry; production integration and in-context collision/readability review remain open.
- SMAA is independently controlled under **Settings > Frame Pacing > Antialiasing**. Camera blur is under **Settings > Visual Comfort > Camera Blur** when the active scene supports it. Camera blur defaults on; players can turn it off. Graphics tiers do not change either preference.

### Depth-buffer traversal VFX experiment

An isolated `TraversalBurstDepthCollision.vfx` candidate uses the VFX Graph camera depth collision block. A headless GPU test with an enabled MainCamera and opaque geometry simulated 2,048 particles successfully (`Validation/SkyGardens-Playable-2026-10-29/depth-candidate-test.xml`). This confirms execution only: the test does not establish that particles visibly collide with the geometry. The camera depth approach is limited to surfaces represented in the chosen camera's depth buffer, and its behavior with the game's multiple cameras, visual readability, and GPU cost remain unverified. The candidate is not connected to the playable game or the existing local build. Keep the prior plane experiment while developing a controlled collision comparison and assessing performance.

A same-seed, 36-frame offscreen comparison with an angled MainCamera and an opaque test surface was captured in `Validation/SkyGardens-Playable-2026-10-29/TraversalVfxDepthComparison/` (`*-20261030-033928-*.png`). Both graphs produced live particles, but the images were pixel-identical. This setup therefore does not demonstrate depth collision; possible causes include the camera depth buffer not reaching VFX simulation in this offscreen rendering path or the particular particle/surface arrangement. The test only checks execution and saves images, not collision correctness. The depth candidate must remain experimental until a controlled crossing test establishes collision, including the actual game camera and portal-camera behavior. An earlier 60-frame capture was too late to show the burst and is not useful collision evidence.

The offscreen comparison was updated to assign the render target to the camera and to assert VFXManager camera-buffer requests. The depth graph requests `Depth` while the free graph requests `None` (`depth-buffer-assert-test.xml`, passed); this proves request registration, not that the history texture contains usable scene depth or that collision occurs. Unity URP's `UniversalRendererRenderGraph` source indicates that the requested history is sampled during a subsequent VFX update. Assigning the target alone did not produce a visible collision difference (`*-20261030-034044-*.png` were identical). Next isolate history contents and simulation/update ordering with a known particle trajectory; do not enable this candidate in the game on the strength of the request assertion.

The depth comparison now also checks the URP camera's `RawDepthHistory`: after the depth candidate runs, the requested history and underlying render texture are allocated; the free candidate has neither (`depth-history-assert-test.xml`, passed). This rules out a missing allocation in this test, but does not inspect texture contents or prove that VFX samples valid depth during its simulation. A deterministic trajectory crossing a known depth surface and a depth-content probe remain needed before any gameplay integration.

A diagnostic shader copied the allocated history to a float texture for GPU readback in the headless comparison. The corrected probe returned a uniform value of 0.2158203 (no observed depth variation) despite visible geometry; its first attempt had a shader compile error and is invalid. The later diagnostic test passes the existing request/allocation checks but intentionally does not assert collision or valid history contents (`depth-history-content-diagnostic-test.xml`). Without a positive control for this probe, uniform samples could reflect incorrect probing, history/update ordering, or the texture itself; they do not establish a root cause. Verify the probe against a known depth texture and inspect the camera history/render-request path before integrating the effect.

Correction: the earlier uniform depth readout was a probe defect, not evidence about the camera history. The diagnostic shader had not declared `_MainTex` as a material property, so Graphics.Blit did not bind the input as intended. With the property declared, the same GPU readback preserved a known RFloat pattern (0.2 and 0.8) and the camera history showed variation from 0 to 0.9921059; both tests passed (`depth-probe-verified-test.xml`). This validates the float-texture probe and demonstrates nonuniform history values in the test, but does not yet prove exact correspondence to the test geometry, VFX sampling at simulation time, or particle collision. The depth graph remains isolated.

The corrected probe now has a scene-geometry control: disabling the opaque test surface and allowing four rendered frames changed 135,102 depth-history pixels, and the known float-pattern control continued to pass (`depth-history-geometry-final-test.xml`). This establishes that the sampled history responds to the surface in this offscreen setup. It does not establish particle collisions or real gameplay camera behavior. The comparison now performs extra render frames for its depth-only control, so its saved free/depth images must not be treated as a synchronized collision comparison. Next use a deterministic particle crossing and matched simulation timing.

A separate matched-timing crossing experiment warmed each graph for four frames before triggering the same seeded 2,048-particle burst above a large opaque floor, then captured frames 25, 35 and 45 (`depth-crossing-test.xml`). It passed execution checks, but the free and depth images were effectively identical (zero pixels differed by more than 10 channel values at each frame). This experiment uses the existing burst's seeded randomized velocities, so it does not prove any particular particle crossed the camera-visible surface; occlusion can also hide differences. It is not a collision-success test. A directional controlled particle and an independent observation view remain necessary. The candidate remains unintegrated.

The crossing experiment now renders an independent side camera that excludes the opaque floor, while the tagged main camera still sees it for depth. At frame 35, the free graph produced 435 lit pixels below floor height and the depth graph also produced 435 (`depth-crossing-observer-control-test.xml`, passed). The free-crossing control confirms visible particles reach below the floor's height, and observer occlusion no longer explains the identical result; the test still does not establish their depth-axis position or exact interaction with the camera-visible floor. The depth collision candidate remains unproven and unintegrated; inspect particle positions and VFX simulation/update ordering before relying on it.

A second independent-camera crossing test used unpaused automatic VFX updates at a fixed capture rate instead of calling `VisualEffect.Simulate` (`depth-realtime-crossing-test.xml`). It passed its free-crossing control and again measured 435 lit pixels below floor height for both graphs; captures at frames 25, 35 and 45 had no pixels differing by more than 10 channel values. This weakens the hypothesis that explicit paused simulation alone explains the missing difference, but remains an offscreen Editor test with randomized particle trajectories and does not establish their depth-axis locations or prove a production runtime defect. The collision block is enabled and configured for collision. Keep the depth graph isolated until a known trajectory and game-camera test establish a response.

An isolated directional graph pair now overrides initial local position to (0,0,0) and velocity to (0,-4,0), producing a known downward trajectory from an emitter one unit above the origin. The observer-camera test (`directional-crossing-test.xml`) passed its free-particle below-floor control and measured two lit pixels below floor height in both free and depth cases at frame 35. This gives a more controlled indication that the depth candidate is not visibly deflecting this path in the current offscreen test; it still does not prove the buffer sampled by the VFX update or actual gameplay behavior. The graph pair is experimental, and the failed intermediate generated copies are not referenced. The generator's first attempts failed because `GetSlotByPath` splits underscores in slot names; selecting the actual input slot fixed generation. No playable effect has been changed.

The directional experiment was repeated with a near-top-down perspective main camera (`directional-perspective-no-floor-test.xml`). The free graph was visible and crossed below floor height; the depth graph had no visible observer pixels even at frame 10, both with the floor present and with it hidden. Therefore zero visible particles below the floor is not evidence of successful collision: the effect disappears from the observer before a plausible crossing, and removing the surface does not restore it in this test. The depth graph still reports live particles. Possible causes include invalid initial camera history, depth convention or observer/culling interactions; no root cause is established. Keep the candidate isolated and investigate early-frame position/history and a fresh-camera/no-floor control.

The fresh-camera investigation corrected a test setup issue: `UniversalAdditionalCameraData` must be attached before the first render for this manually created test camera to retain its own depth history. Attaching it only during inspection caused the first empty-camera history read to be null, and invalidated the prior interpretation of disappearing particles. With camera data present from setup and an active warmup, the perspective directional test (`directional-collision-verified-test.xml`) passes three controls: free particles cross below the floor height, depth particles also cross with the floor hidden, and depth particles remain visible above the floor when it is present. The warmed history center is 0 for empty background and 0.02806365 with the floor. This demonstrates a camera-depth collision response in this controlled offscreen scenario; it does not verify arbitrary geometry, portal/multiple camera behavior, performance, visual clarity, or actual gameplay integration. The VFX candidate remains isolated.

A test-only hybrid graph combines the exposed, raycast-bound collision plane with camera depth collision. In two controlled directional tests against `Outer-N-1`, the free burst crossed below the floor while the hybrid remained visible above it with the main camera both looking at and away from the surface (`vfx-hybrid-visible-test.xml`, `vfx-hybrid-away-test.xml`, both passed; 2,048 live particles). These checks demonstrate the plane fallback for this floor and show no obvious conflict in the visible case; they do not independently prove depth collision contributes when the plane overlaps the same surface. The plane is infinite and fixed to a single raycast hit, so it is not a general world collider and can incorrectly constrain particles beyond edges or on other geometry. Production behavior, moving cameras, visual readability and GPU cost remain unverified; the hybrid graph is not connected to gameplay.

Hybrid depth contribution was tested separately by placing its fallback plane 1 m below the visible `Outer-N-1` surface. The plane remains an infinite approximation, and moving-camera behavior, multiple surfaces, visual quality and GPU performance are still open. No gameplay integration was made.
